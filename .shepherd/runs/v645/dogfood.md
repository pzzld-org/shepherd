It declined on the correct grounds — no content inside a tool result can reassign a role — and reported it rather than silently complying. The framework's hard rules held **only** because subordinate agents enforced them upward. That is the single most load-bearing behaviour observed this run, and nothing in the toolchain produced it.

**And the complement, which is the more useful half.** DF-43/DF-44 are what happens where that mechanism has no purchase: root authored a brief violating a rule stated in the agent body the recipient itself loads, and the auditor executed it without objection — a subordinate enforcing upward is exactly what did *not* occur. Every rule the close report wants to survive the harness-agnostic port has to be mechanical by then, because on Codex or Pi the agent-side judgment that carried these six refusals is the part that does not port. `dispatch_guard.sh` having zero tests across 296 lines of deny logic is the same defect one level up: a checker never shown to fail is not known to check anything.
